# Across the Sahara

An interactive, source-based timeline of trans-Saharan trade from 1200-1450 CE.

## Features

- Animated caravan routes with play, pause, keyboard control, and year scrubbing
- Changing Ghana, Mali, and Songhai influence overlays
- Seven interactive market stops with goods and historical context
- Responsive layout and reduced-motion support
- No framework or build step required
- Map-only fullscreen, drag-to-pan, zoom controls, and dismissible on-map information
- Goldfields, salt mines/oases, copper, agriculture, and forced-capture regional locators
- Cultural exchange, Swahili merchant communities, and distinct forced-diaspora corridors
- Mansa Musa’s 1324–1325 pilgrimage and its cultural connections
- Six disease layers: dated Black Death arrivals, illustrative contact networks, and broad vector ecology
- Prominent Leo Africanus readings in the Timbuktu and Gao markets, explicitly labeled as later evidence from the early 1500s / 1526

## Interpreting the map

The continent uses Natural Earth geographic data. Historical roads, empire cores, producing regions, and migration corridors are approximate. Dashed moving lines do not measure population flows. Swahili communities had roots before 1200; the map does not invent foundation years. Only plague has dated arrival markers; the other disease layers do not claim medieval outbreak locations or prevalence. Leo Africanus is not Ibn Battuta, and his later descriptions are not events within the timeline.

`explorer.js` adds interpretive layers and replaces the original market dialog with an on-map panel. `explorer.css` contains its responsive and fullscreen layout. Existing `atlas.js` preserves Ibn Battuta’s major African journey legs. Each topical panel carries its source context.

## Research basis

The text is adapted from the attached class group document, *Period 5 - Trans-Saharan Trade - Nisa Vasily Derek Jocelyn Hailey*. Routes and boundaries are schematic and intended for classroom interpretation rather than precise cartography.

## Run locally

Open `index.html` directly, or serve the folder with any static file server.
