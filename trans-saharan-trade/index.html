The system behind the journey

A network engineered for distance

Trade flourished because technologies, financial practices, and states worked together across an extreme landscape.

01
⌁

Camels & caravans

Camels could travel far with little water and carry heavier loads. Saddles distributed weight; organized caravans improved efficiency and protection.

02
◇

Credit & currency

Credit let merchants deposit money and withdraw it elsewhere. Gold and salt served long-distance exchange; cowrie shells supported local trade.

03
♜

States & security

Ghana, Mali, and Songhai taxed trade, protected routes, maintained armies, and supported lodging and markets for visiting merchants.

04
✦

Crops & environments

Wheat and cotton moved from North to West Africa, while dates grown at Saharan oases sustained travelers. Trade joined desert, savanna, river, and forest zones.

05
◉

Faith & scholarship

Merchants and rulers helped spread Islam and learning. Mansa Musa built mosques, supported schools, and brought teachers to Mali, while local practices continued.

06
△

Disease & risk

Crowded markets and mobile caravans could spread smallpox, measles, leprosy, and other illnesses. Disease and animal loss could disrupt cities and merchants.

Research note

Built from the group’s work

The market descriptions and historical timeline synthesize the attached “Trans-Saharan Trade” group document. The experience emphasizes the group’s research on transportation, commerce, empire, cities, exchanged goods, cultural diffusion, disease, and a Moroccan diplomat’s account of West Africa.

Routes and boundaries are intentionally schematic: they show connections and changing influence, not modern borders or precise territorial claims. Disease evidence for this period can be incomplete, so the project presents transmission as a historical risk rather than claiming a precise outbreak map.
